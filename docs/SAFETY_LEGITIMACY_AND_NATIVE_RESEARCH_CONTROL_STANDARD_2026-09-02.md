# Safety, Legitimacy, and Native Research Control Standard

**Project:** AiByz / AEGIS-AI-LAB  
**Effective date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** Mandatory engineering control

## 1. Purpose

This standard establishes a hard publication and research boundary for native archaeology, reverse engineering, replay analysis, and external research performed for this project.

The project may study machine behavior when doing so is necessary for engineering understanding, but the repository must not become a distribution channel for proprietary game binaries, copied game source, or other material whose publication basis has not been established.

## 2. Non-Negotiable Rule

**Do not publish information or code taken from the game's executable merely because Ghidra, a debugger, a parser, or another tool made it observable.**

Tool output does not create ownership or publication rights.

The project therefore separates:

1. **Research observation** — material examined privately to answer an engineering question.
2. **Engineering knowledge** — original descriptions, measurements, hypotheses, and experimentally established behavior.
3. **Publication material** — content approved for repository distribution under an established rights basis.

Only category 3 may enter the public repository.

## 3. Native Executable Boundary

The game's executable and associated proprietary application material are treated as third-party/proprietary unless an explicit contrary publication right is established.

The following are prohibited from publication by default:

- executable copies;
- DLLs or other proprietary binaries;
- proprietary assets;
- copied or substantially reproduced native source;
- private symbols or symbol packages;
- substantial decompiler output reproducing the executable's implementation;
- bulk disassembly or pseudocode derived from proprietary implementation;
- extracted proprietary tables or data dumps when their publication basis is unclear;
- credentials, private information, or security-sensitive material.

A Ghidra project file is not automatically safe merely because it is an analysis artifact. It may contain proprietary-derived representations and must therefore remain outside the public repository unless separately reviewed.

## 4. What May Be Published

Subject to the existing publication/rights standard, acceptable research artifacts generally include:

- original engineering explanations;
- original architectural diagrams;
- experiment procedures;
- measurements and observations generated by our own experiments;
- hashes and provenance metadata;
- addresses or ranges used as private research references, where publication creates no material reproduction of proprietary implementation;
- short, necessary excerpts only when a documented publication basis exists;
- original pseudocode or structural descriptions that do not reproduce substantial proprietary implementation;
- parser methodology and limitations;
- independently authored behavioral specifications;
- negative results and uncertainty statements;
- citations to legitimate public documentation or sources.

When in doubt, publish the **conclusion and method**, not the proprietary artifact from which the conclusion was derived.

## 5. Ghidra-Specific Controls

Ghidra is an investigation instrument, not a rights-clearing mechanism.

Every native-analysis result must be classified before publication:

### A. Safe engineering result

An independently authored statement of observed behavior, measurement, architecture, or hypothesis that does not reproduce protected implementation.

### B. Restricted research evidence

Decompiler output, raw disassembly, extracted data, internal strings, binary structures, or other executable-derived evidence retained privately for verification.

### C. Prohibited publication material

Anything that would amount to distributing proprietary implementation, substantial copied code, bulk binary-derived contents, or material whose rights status is unresolved.

Category B evidence may support an engineering conclusion without being published itself.

## 6. Evidence Discipline

Native evidence must retain its provenance and confidence classification.

The project uses the following distinction:

**Observed:** directly established by an approved experiment or authoritative artifact.  
**Inferred:** supported by multiple observations but not directly demonstrated.  
**Hypothesis:** plausible explanation requiring further testing.  
**Historical:** preserved for provenance but not treated as current runtime truth.

A string, symbol name, decompiler label, or address is not by itself proof of semantics.

A parser observation is not automatically proof of internal engine state.

A command being issued is not proof that the engine executed it successfully.

## 7. Research Workflow

For every material native question:

1. Define the engineering question.
2. Establish why native evidence is necessary.
3. Acquire or inspect the minimum necessary evidence.
4. Keep proprietary-derived artifacts in the controlled research environment.
5. Record hashes and provenance where appropriate.
6. Separate direct observation from inference.
7. Write an original engineering interpretation.
8. Apply the publication/rights gate independently from the evidence gate.
9. Publish only the cleared engineering artifact.
10. Preserve restricted evidence privately for reproducibility where permitted.

## 8. No Automatic Publication

No native-analysis artifact is presumed publishable because:

- it was generated automatically;
- it is only a few lines long;
- it came from an open-source analysis tool;
- it contains only strings;
- it is decompiler-generated rather than hand-written;
- it appears in a public executable;
- similar information exists elsewhere online.

Each case requires its own publication determination.

## 9. External Research

External documentation, repositories, articles, parser projects, and datasets must retain their source and provenance.

Third-party material is not copied into the repository merely for convenience. Prefer links, citations, original summaries, and reproducible procedures.

If licensing or redistribution rights are unclear, the project records the source and uses an original description rather than republishing the material.

## 10. Legal/Compliance Boundary

This engineering standard is intentionally conservative, but it is not a legal opinion.

Questions involving copyright exceptions, reverse-engineering rights, license terms, contracts, interoperability provisions, jurisdiction-specific law, or redistribution rights may require qualified legal review.

The engineering rule is simple: **when rights are unclear, do not publish the material.**

## 11. Repository Gate

Before any native-derived artifact is committed to the public repository, the engineer must be able to answer:

- What is the source?
- Who owns or controls the source?
- What exactly are we publishing?
- Is the published material original engineering work or reproduced third-party material?
- What is the publication basis?
- Could the artifact materially substitute for proprietary implementation?
- Does it contain copied code, bulk data, private information, or security-sensitive information?
- Can the engineering conclusion be communicated without publishing the restricted evidence?

If any material answer is unresolved, the artifact remains private.

## 12. History and Remediation

The repository's current tree and Git history are separate publication surfaces.

Removing a file from the current tree does not establish that its historical blobs are gone.

Any historical remediation must therefore use:

**audit → classify → manifest → rewrite → fresh-clone verification → publication certification.**

Blind destruction of project history is prohibited because provenance itself is valuable engineering evidence.

## 13. Engineer's Operating Principle

The project optimizes for understanding without unnecessary disclosure.

We will use the strongest evidence necessary to answer the engineering question, retain restricted evidence where appropriate, derive original knowledge from it, and publish only what has an established basis for publication.

**Responsible engineering means knowing not only what can be discovered, but what should—and should not—be distributed.**
