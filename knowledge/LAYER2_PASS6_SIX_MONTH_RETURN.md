## Core methodological decision

The atomic empirical unit remains the **decision opportunity**, not the action alone.

A recorded action tells us what happened.

A decision opportunity lets us ask whether another materially feasible action would have been better given the information actually available at that time.

Therefore the minimum research tuple is:

`state + information + beliefs + objective + feasible alternatives + selected action + outcome`.

## Epistemic protection

Never perform retrospective evaluation as though the actor knew later information.

Maintain two views:

- `AS_KNOWN_THEN` — information legitimately available at the decision point;
- `AS_KNOWN_NOW` — later information available to the analyst.

Only the first may be used to judge the rationality of the original decision. The second is useful for explaining why the decision succeeded or failed.

## What each construct must eventually prove

### Resource deficit
Can projected resource availability predict whether a desired conversion can complete by a deadline?

### Shadow value
Does marginal resource value vary predictably with feasible alternatives and timing?

### Capability readiness
Can time-to-useful-effect be predicted more accurately than raw unit presence?

### Capability reliability
Does estimated probability of accomplishing an objective calibrate against repeated outcomes?

### Sustainability
Can the controller predict how long a capability can be maintained?

### Production latency
Can strategic demand-to-usable-capability time be predicted?

### Bottleneck
Can relaxing the identified limiting constraint measurably increase desired capability throughput?

### Timing window
Can the model identify intervals where action value is materially elevated?

### Initiative
Can decision pressure be measured through response demand and restriction of viable alternatives?

### Optionality
Does the weighted value of remaining feasible transitions predict recovery/adaptation value better than raw option count?

### Conversion efficiency
Does objective progress per total strategic cost discriminate superior actions?

### Conversion tax
Does an action materially reduce the opponent's feasible transition value relative to its own cost?

### VOI
Does information gathering improve action quality enough to justify acquisition cost?

### Decision regret
Can counterfactual analysis identify materially superior feasible alternatives without hindsight contamination?

### Failure diagnosticity
Does weighting failures by causal discrimination improve future prediction compared with naive failure updating?

### Robustness
Does a strategy retain acceptable performance across plausible state perturbations?

### Reversibility
Does exit cost predict the appropriate confidence/reserve requirement before commitment?

### Belief staleness
Does confidence degrade predictably as evidence ages under changing state volatility?

### Causal attribution
Can the corpus distinguish action effects from concurrent or delayed causes?

### Decision budget
Can excessive simultaneous commitments predict coordination failure or abandoned objectives?

## Measurement maturity ladder

Every variable must progress through:

`L0 concept -> L1 named -> L2 proxy -> L3 derived -> L4 replay-measurable -> L5 predictive -> L6 calibrated -> L7 policy-useful -> L8 runtime-trusted`

No variable should be promoted by intuition alone.

## Immediate empirical corpus

The next artifact should be a replay-derived decision-opportunity corpus. Prefer diverse, strategically informative games over sheer game count.

Prioritize events involving:

1. strategic transition;
2. resource reallocation;
3. production expansion;
4. technology investment;
5. scouting decisions;
6. attack commitment;
7. retreat/withdrawal;
8. counter-transition;
9. market conversion;
10. recovery after failure.

## Replay selection principle

Do not select only clean victories.

Include:

- wins;
- losses;
- reversals;
- failed attacks;
- successful defensive holds;
- incorrect predictions;
- unexpected transitions;
- ambiguous information;
- execution failures.

A biased corpus will produce a biased doctrine.

## Stock-source boundary

HD/2013 remains the historical strategic archaeology source.

AiBuilder is stock tooling/context and may illuminate exposed parameters and conceptual vocabulary, but it is not to be copied into AEGIS architecture. Small contextual excerpts may be used as attributed exhibits when necessary; large contiguous source reproduction is neither necessary nor the research objective.

Promisory and failed user-derived projects remain outside the strategic evidence lineage.

## Six-month restart exercise

A future engineer should be able to pick one real decision event and answer:

> What was observable?
>
> What was hidden?
>
> What did the actor plausibly believe?
>
> What objective was active?
>
> What constraints applied?
>
> Which transitions were actually feasible?
>
> What alternatives existed?
>
> What was selected?
>
> What commitment did that create?
>
> What result was expected?
>
> What actually happened?
>
> What changed at immediate, short, strategic, and terminal horizons?
>
> Which causal attribution is justified?
>
> What should belief update by?
>
> What would falsify the explanation?

If the repository cannot answer these questions, Pass 6 is incomplete.

## Next pass

The default workflow is:

`replay -> decision opportunities -> operational measurements -> calibration -> counterfactual analysis -> model revision -> controlled experiment`.

Only after this should numerical policy and Byzantine doctrine begin to harden.
